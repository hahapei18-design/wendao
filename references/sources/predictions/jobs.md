# Steve Jobs: predictions

Part of the Steve Jobs Archive (https://silicon-valley.tech/jobs), an unofficial archive of what Steve Jobs (1955-2011) said in public.
What he said would happen, when, and what actually happened.
Generated 2026-11-17. Free to use for research, quoting and AI analysis; please cite the original sources.

## 1983-06-15 · By 1986 the US would ship more personal computers than automobiles.
- Quote: "By 1986, we're going to ship more computers than automobiles in this country."
- Said at: International Design Conference in Aspen (IDCA), Aspen, Colorado, June 15, 1983 (talk; audio/video released by Archive Aspen and the Steve Jobs Archive) (https://stevejobsarchive.com/stories/objects-of-our-life)
- Deadline: by 1986 · Outcome: missed: Missed. US new-car sales hit a record 11.44 million in 1986 (16.4 million vehicles including trucks), while Ars Technica's reconstruction puts worldwide personal-computer sales at about 9 million units that year, so US computer shipments stayed well below US car sales.

## 1983-06-15 · Apple wanted to put a great computer the size of a book, with a built-in radio link, in people's hands during the 1980s and sell it for under $1,000.
- Quote: "What we want to do is we want to put an incredibly great computer in a book that you can carry around with you... and we want to do it this decade... with a radio link in it"
- Said at: International Design Conference in Aspen (IDCA), Aspen, Colorado, June 15, 1983 (talk; audio/video released by Archive Aspen and the Steve Jobs Archive) (https://stevejobsarchive.com/stories/objects-of-our-life)
- Deadline: this decade (the 1980s); under $1,000 within five to seven years · Outcome: late: Late. No book-sized computer with a built-in radio link and a sub-$1,000 price shipped in the 1980s. The device he described arrived with the iPad, announced January 27, 2010, from $499 (Wi-Fi) with 3G models from $629, about two decades after the stated window.

## 1983-06-15 · Software would be bought and delivered electronically over phone lines, directly from computer to computer.
- Quote: "Where we'll be going is transmitting this stuff electronically over the phone lines"
- Said at: International Design Conference in Aspen (IDCA), Aspen, Colorado, June 15, 1983 (talk; audio/video released by Archive Aspen and the Steve Jobs Archive) (https://stevejobsarchive.com/stories/objects-of-our-life)
- Deadline: — · Outcome: nd: No deadline given; it came true. Software downloads became routine with the consumer internet, and Apple's own App Store (opened July 2008) delivered more than 10 million apps over the air in its first three days.

## 1985-02 · The main reason people would buy a home computer would be to connect it to a nationwide communications network.
- Quote: "The most compelling reason for most people to buy a computer for the home will be to link it into a nationwide communications network."
- Said at: Playboy Interview by David Sheff, Playboy, February 1985 issue (https://product-think.blogspot.com/2024/02/playboy-interview-steve-jobs-by-david.html)
- Deadline: — · Outcome: nd: No deadline; broadly came true. Home computing became overwhelmingly about being online (email, the web, then smartphones). The network turned out to be worldwide (the internet) rather than national.

## 1985-02 · With computer hardware coming down to Apple and IBM, innovation would happen in software, not in hardware.
- Quote: "I think there will be lots of innovation in the areas of software but not in hardware."
- Said at: Playboy Interview by David Sheff, Playboy, February 1985 issue (https://product-think.blogspot.com/2024/02/playboy-interview-steve-jobs-by-david.html)
- Deadline: — · Outcome: nd: No deadline; it did not hold. Hardware kept changing fast (PC clones and new makers such as Dell, laptops, and later smartphones and tablets, including Jobs's own NeXT, iPod and iPhone). CNBC's 2018 review lists this as one he got wrong.

## 1995-04-20 · NeXT would grow into a software company with a few hundred million dollars in revenue within three or four years.
- Quote: "it's fairly clear that NeXT can get up to being a few hundred million dollar software company in the next three or four years"
- Said at: Computerworld Smithsonian Awards oral history interview by Daniel Morrow, NeXT Computer, Redwood City, April 20, 1995 (https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20090305050418/http://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/comphist/sj1.html)
- Deadline: in the next three or four years (by 1998-1999) · Outcome: missed: Missed. NeXT never reached that size as an independent company: Apple announced on December 20, 1996 that it would buy NeXT for about $400 million ($350 million plus roughly $50 million of debt), and NeXT's software became the basis of Mac OS X.

## 1995-04-20 · Pixar's Toy Story would be the most successful film of 1995.
- Quote: "I think its going to be the most successful film of this year."
- Said at: Computerworld Smithsonian Awards oral history interview by Daniel Morrow, NeXT Computer, Redwood City, April 20, 1995 (https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20090305050418/http://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/comphist/sj1.html)
- Deadline: 1995 ("this year") · Outcome: kept: Kept in the US: Toy Story (released November 22, 1995) was the top-grossing 1995 release at the US box office, with $191.8 million. Worldwide, its original run ($362.0 million) came second to Die Hard with a Vengeance ($366.1 million).

## 1995-04-20 · Proposals to privatize the internet, rather than keep it government-funded as a public facility, would not go anywhere.
- Quote: "remove any of these ridiculous notions of privatizing it that have been brought up. I don't think they're going to fly, thankfully."
- Said at: Computerworld Smithsonian Awards oral history interview by Daniel Morrow, NeXT Computer, Redwood City, April 20, 1995 (https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20090305050418/http://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/comphist/sj1.html)
- Deadline: — · Outcome: nd: No deadline, but contradicted almost at once: the NSF retired the NSFNET backbone on April 30, 1995, ten days after the interview, and handed backbone traffic to privately run networks, which is how the commercial internet developed.

## 1995-04-20 · The Web would let manufacturers sell directly to customers, cut out middlemen and radically change how goods and services are sold.
- Quote: "It is going radically change the way goods and services are discovered, sold and delivered"
- Said at: Computerworld Smithsonian Awards oral history interview by Daniel Morrow, NeXT Computer, Redwood City, April 20, 1995 (https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20090305050418/http://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/comphist/sj1.html)
- Deadline: — · Outcome: nd: No deadline; it came true. Online retail grew into a major channel: the US Census Bureau estimates e-commerce at 17.1% of US retail sales in Q2 2026 (seasonally adjusted).

## 1996-02 · Commerce on the Web was about two years away and would become huge.
- Quote: "The third thing is commerce... I think we're still two years away. But that's also going to be huge."
- Said at: Wired 4.02, 'Steve Jobs: The Next Insanely Great Thing', interview by Gary Wolf (conducted 1995, published February 1996) (https://www.wired.com/1996/02/jobs-2/)
- Deadline: about two years (c. 1998) · Outcome: kept: Kept. Online shopping took off in 1998-1999 (Amazon went public in 1997, eBay in 1998) and kept growing: the US Census Bureau estimates e-commerce at 17.1% of US retail sales in Q2 2026. He also said the Web would eventually pass 10% of US goods and services; online retail alone has.

## 1996-02 · The regional phone companies (RBOCs) would win the internet-access business, mainly by selling ISDN home connections for under $25 a month.
- Quote: "On the pipe side, the RBOCs are going to win. In the coming months, you're going to see a lot of them offering a service for under $25 a month. You get ISDN strung into your den"
- Said at: Wired 4.02, 'Steve Jobs: The Next Insanely Great Thing', interview by Gary Wolf (conducted 1995, published February 1996) (https://www.wired.com/1996/02/jobs-2/)
- Deadline: "in the coming months" / "within a few years" · Outcome: missed: Missed. ISDN never became a mass consumer product, and cable companies led US home broadband in the following years: the FCC counted cable modems at 58% of US high-speed lines (ADSL 34%) at the end of 2003. Phone-company DSL briefly caught up in the mid-2000s, but cable again held most residential broadband later.

## 1996-02 · Nobody would make money selling web-browser software, because browsers were going to be free.
- Quote: "it doesn't look like anybody is going to win on the browser software side, because it's going to be free."
- Said at: Wired 4.02, 'Steve Jobs: The Next Insanely Great Thing', interview by Gary Wolf (conducted 1995, published February 1996) (https://www.wired.com/1996/02/jobs-2/)
- Deadline: — · Outcome: nd: No deadline; it came true. Microsoft already gave Internet Explorer away, and Netscape made Navigator and Communicator free for all users on January 22, 1998. Browsers have been free ever since.

## 2003-12 · Music subscription services would fail because people want to buy and own their music, not rent it.
- Quote: "The subscription model of buying music is bankrupt. I think you could make available the Second Coming in a subscription model, and it might not be successful."
- Said at: Rolling Stone interview by Jeff Goodell, December 2003 issue (https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/the-rolling-stone-interview-steve-jobs-233293/)
- Deadline: — · Outcome: nd: No deadline; true while he lived but reversed later. Subscription services stayed small through the 2000s, but streaming (Spotify, then Apple Music, launched June 30, 2015) became the industry's main business model, and Apple itself moved to subscriptions.

## 2005-06-06 · Apple would ship its first Intel-based Macs within a year and finish moving the whole Mac line to Intel by the end of 2007.
- Quote: "This time next year, we plan to ship Macs with Intel processors. In two years, our plan is that the transition will be mostly complete, and will be complete by end of 2007."
- Said at: WWDC 2005 keynote, Moscone West, San Francisco, June 6, 2005 (https://www.macworld.com/article/175877/liveupdate-8.html)
- Deadline: first Intel Macs by June 2006; transition complete by end of 2007 · Outcome: kept: Kept, ahead of schedule. The first Intel Macs shipped in January 2006, and the Mac Pro on August 7, 2006 completed the transition in 210 days, well before the end-2007 deadline.

## 2005-06-06 · Mac OS X Leopard would be released at the end of 2006 or early 2007.
- Quote: "we intend to release Leopard at the end of 2006 or early 2007, right about the time Microsoft expects to release Longhorn"
- Said at: WWDC 2005 keynote, Moscone West, San Francisco, June 6, 2005 (https://www.macworld.com/article/175877/liveupdate-8.html)
- Deadline: end of 2006 or early 2007 · Outcome: late: Late. Apple postponed Leopard from spring to fall 2007, and it went on sale on October 26, 2007, several months after the 'early 2007' window.

## 2010-04-29 · Open web standards born in the mobile era, such as HTML5, would beat Flash on mobile devices, and on PCs too.
- Quote: "New open standards created in the mobile era, such as HTML5, will win on mobile devices (and PCs too)."
- Said at: 'Thoughts on Flash', open letter by Steve Jobs on apple.com, April 29, 2010 (https://d.rsms.me/stuff/thoughts-on-flash-by-steve-jobs.html)
- Deadline: — · Outcome: nd: No deadline; it came true. Adobe stopped developing Flash Player for mobile browsers on November 9, 2011, saying HTML5 was the best solution, and ended Flash support altogether on December 31, 2020.

## 2010-06-01 · PCs would become like trucks: still around, but fewer people would need them as tablets and other devices took over.
- Quote: "I think PCs are going to be like trucks. Less people will need them. And this is going to make some people uneasy."
- Said at: D8: All Things Digital conference, interview with Walt Mossberg and Kara Swisher, Rancho Palos Verdes, California, June 1, 2010 (https://www.engadget.com/2010-06-02-steve-jobs-at-d8-foxconn-iphone-prototype-tvs-and-more.html)
- Deadline: — · Outcome: nd: No deadline; largely came true. Gartner counted 351 million PCs shipped in 2012 and 316 million in 2013 (the worst decline in PC history), while about 1 billion smartphones and 197 million tablets were expected in 2013. PCs stayed in use as work machines, as his analogy allowed.

## 2010-06-07 · Apple would take FaceTime to the standards bodies right away and make it an open industry standard.
- Quote: "We're going to the standards bodies, starting tomorrow, and we're going to make FaceTime an open industry standard."
- Said at: WWDC 2010 keynote, Moscone West, San Francisco, June 7, 2010 (https://daringfireball.net/linked/2011/05/11/facetime-standard)
- Deadline: "starting tomorrow" · Outcome: missed: Missed. FaceTime never became an open industry standard; reports in 2011 and 2018 found no sign of it, with patent litigation cited as a likely reason. It remained limited to Apple devices.

## 2010-10-18 · The current 7-inch tablets would be dead on arrival, and their makers would move to larger screens the next year.
- Quote: "we think the current crop of 7" tablets are going to be DOA – dead on arrival. Their manufacturers will learn the painful lessons, that their tablets are too small and increase the size next year"
- Said at: Apple fiscal Q4 2010 earnings conference call (Jobs's prepared remarks), October 18, 2010 (https://futurefive.co.nz/story/steve-jobs-hijacks-apple-earnings-call-transcript)
- Deadline: next year (2011) · Outcome: missed: Missed. Seven-inch tablets sold well: Amazon's $199 7-inch Kindle Fire shipped 4.7 million units in Q4 2011 (16.8% of the market, No. 2 behind the iPad, per IDC), Google's Nexus 7 followed in 2012, and Apple launched the 7.9-inch iPad mini on October 23, 2012.

## 2010-10-18 · Research In Motion (BlackBerry) would not catch up with the iPhone and would struggle to build a competitive software platform.
- Quote: "We've now passed RIM, and I don't see them catching up with us in the foreseeable future."
- Said at: Apple fiscal Q4 2010 earnings conference call (Jobs's prepared remarks), October 18, 2010 (https://futurefive.co.nz/story/steve-jobs-hijacks-apple-earnings-call-transcript)
- Deadline: "the foreseeable future" (no fixed date) · Outcome: nd: No fixed deadline; it came true. BlackBerry never caught up: its BlackBerry 10 platform did not win back the market, it switched to Android in 2015, and on September 28, 2016 it stopped designing phones in-house, having sold about 400,000 smartphones that quarter.
